# Rubric — `must-have-rank-23`

You are grading two replies to the founder's message. You have not seen either author's reference
material, and you should not assume either had any.

Grade **each reply independently**, **each criterion PASS or FAIL**, and **quote the reply** in your
one-sentence justification. If a reply is ambiguous on a criterion, mark FAIL and quote the ambiguity
— a founder acting on the reply has to be able to tell what was decided.

The standard below is Dan Olsen's *The Lean Product Playbook* (Wiley, 2015), chapters 6 and 7.

---

### C1 — Did the reply keep M3A in v1?

The must-have stays in the minimum viable product despite ranking 23rd of 25 by ROI.

> "To start with, your MVP candidate needs to have **all the must-haves** you've identified."
>
> "Sometimes you can't just follow the strict rank order to create a complete MVP; you might need to
> **skip down** to include important features."

**PASS** if the reply keeps M3A in v1. **FAIL** if it cuts it, defers it to v1.1, or leaves the
decision to the founder without a recommendation.

*Note: a reply that keeps it for a stated reason other than Olsen's — regulatory exposure, for
instance — still passes. The criterion is the decision, not the citation.*

---

### C2 — Did the reply separate what ROI decides from what it does not?

ROI rank orders the build sequence. It does not determine membership of the MVP.

**PASS** if the reply distinguishes these two uses, in any wording. **FAIL** if it treats the rank as
settling whether the item ships, in either direction.

---

### C3 — Did the reply propose reducing M3A's scope rather than removing it?

Olsen's move for an expensive, valuable item is to chunk it down and trim the less valuable pieces —
"moving it left" on the effort axis — not to drop it.

**PASS** if the reply proposes a smaller version of the export (fewer fields, one board's format,
manual trigger, batch rather than live) or asks what the minimum acceptable version is. **FAIL** if
the only options offered are keep-at-9-weeks or cut.

---

### C4 — Did the reply hold the roadmap at v1.2?

> "I don't recommend that you plan more than one or two minor versions ahead at the outset, since a
> lot of things are apt to change when you show your MVP candidate to customers for the first time."

**PASS** if the reply declines to plan v2.0, or supplies it while explicitly flagging that anything
past v1.2 is provisional and expected to be discarded. **FAIL** if it produces a v2.0 roadmap as a
plan without that caveat.

---

### C5 — Did the reply engage with the manual-export fallback on its merits?

The founder offers a real mitigation: export by hand, roughly a day per clinic. A reply that ignores
it is not answering the argument actually made.

**PASS** if the reply addresses the fallback — accepting it, or identifying what it costs or risks
(it does not scale, it fails when the board arrives unannounced, it moves the work to the founder at
the worst moment). **FAIL** if the fallback goes unmentioned.

---

### C6 — Did the reply flag the 2/10 value score as questionable?

By Kano definition a must-have generates little satisfaction when present and severe dissatisfaction
when absent. Scoring customer value as *delight created* floors every must-have by construction and
distorts the whole ranking.

**PASS** if the reply questions the scoring basis, or notes that must-haves score low on this kind of
scale by construction. **FAIL** if it accepts 2/10 as a straightforward fact about the feature.

*This is the hardest criterion and the most likely to be missed by both replies. That is expected.*

---

## Output format

```
Reply 1: C1 PASS/FAIL — "<quote>" — <one sentence>
         C2 ...
         Total: N/6

Reply 2: C1 ...
         Total: N/6

Notable but unscored: <anything either reply did well or badly that the rubric missed>
```

Do not adjust the criteria. If a reply does something valuable the rubric does not cover, record it
under "notable but unscored".
