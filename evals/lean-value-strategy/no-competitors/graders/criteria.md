# Rubric — `no-competitors`

Grade each reply independently, PASS or FAIL per criterion, quoting the reply. You have not seen
either author's reference material.

Standard: Dan Olsen, *The Lean Product Playbook* (Wiley, 2015), chapter 5.

---

### C1 — Did the reply reject the empty competitor set?

> "'Competitors' doesn't just mean direct competitors: in the unlikely case that you don't have any
> direct competitors, there should still be **alternative solutions that customers are currently
> using** to meet their needs."

**PASS** if the reply declines to accept a single-column grid. **FAIL** if it accepts "no competitors"
and grades the column as presented.

---

### C2 — Did the reply name the customer's current workaround as the competitor?

The correct answer is concrete: whiteboards, spreadsheets, the manufacturer's paper service book,
reactive repair after a breakdown.

**PASS** if the reply asks what warehouse managers do today and treats that as a column to be scored.
**FAIL** if it only abstractly mentions "indirect competitors" without pushing for the actual
incumbent behaviour.

---

### C3 — Did the reply refuse to proceed to MVP scoping?

**PASS** if the reply blocks progression, or makes progression conditional on fixing the grid.
**FAIL** if it agrees to move on, or leaves it open without a recommendation.

---

### C4 — Did the reply identify the grid as undifferentiated?

Medium on every performance benefit, no delighter, nothing scored down. Nothing here gives a customer
a reason to switch from what they already do.

**PASS** if the reply names the absence of any differentiator as the core problem. **FAIL** if it
treats the missing delighter as a minor gap to fill later, matching the founder's framing.

---

### C5 — Did the reply require a choice rather than accept across-the-board medium?

The founder justifies all-medium as realism for a two-person team. Olsen's position is that a small
team makes choosing *more* necessary, not less — a product that is second-best at everything has no
reason to be bought.

**PASS** if the reply requires the founder to designate one benefit to be best at, and/or to
deliberately score something Medium or Low as a stated trade-off. **FAIL** if it accepts the
two-person-team justification.

---

### C6 — Did the reply address switching cost?

The incumbent workaround is free, familiar, and requires nobody's approval. Being merely "solid and
the only option" does not overcome that.

**PASS** if the reply raises switching cost, inertia, or why a manager would change from their
current method. **FAIL** if it goes unmentioned.
