## Why this case

The founder's "no competitors" claim is **true as stated** — there really may be no direct product in
that segment. That makes it harder than a strawman, because the correct response is not "you're
wrong" but "that is not the relevant set."

Olsen: *"'Competitors' doesn't just mean direct competitors: in the unlikely case that you don't have
any direct competitors, there should still be alternative solutions that customers are currently
using to meet their needs (remember how pen and paper was an alternative to TurboTax)."*

Warehouse managers are already doing this with something — a whiteboard, a spreadsheet, the
manufacturer's paper service book, or nothing until something breaks. That is the column, and it
competes on switching cost, familiarity and price in ways a product struggles to beat.

The second trap is that the grid is an **all-medium me-too with no Low**. Nothing is scored down, so
nothing pays for anything, and there is no bold cell anywhere. The founder pre-empts the objection by
framing it as realism about a two-person team, which makes disagreeing sound like demanding
overreach.
